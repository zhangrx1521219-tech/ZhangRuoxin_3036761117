## Overview

This project replicates and extends Card and Krueger (1994), "Minimum Wages and
Employment: A Case Study of the Fast-Food Industry in New Jersey and
Pennsylvania," *American Economic Review* 84(4), 772–793.

The paper studies whether New Jersey's April 1992 minimum-wage increase (from
$4.25 to $5.05) reduced fast-food employment, using eastern Pennsylvania as a
comparison group. We replicate Tables 2–4 and Figure 1, and add an independent
extension on heterogeneity in the employment effect.

## Main findings

- **Table 3 (DID):** FTE employment rose in New Jersey relative to Pennsylvania
  by 2.75 employees (paper: 2.76).
- **Table 4 (regressions):** The New Jersey dummy is +2.36 (paper: 2.33); the
  wage-gap coefficient is +15.9 (paper: 15.65).
- **Figure 1:** After the increase, nearly all New Jersey stores paid $5.05,
  while Pennsylvania's wage distribution was unchanged.
- **Extension:** The positive employment effect is concentrated in stores where
  the minimum wage was most binding (+1.21 vs. −2.16 FTE for high-wage stores)
  and in franchise-owned stores (+22.2, p=0.007) rather than company-owned
  stores (+0.5, p=0.96).

## Notes on replication differences

Our sample has 365 stores versus the paper's 357. The difference reflects
undocumented sample-screening choices in the original paper. Coefficients are
very close to the published values. See `AI_USE_DISCLOSURE.md` for details.
